GPU Supply, Custom Chips, and Datacenter Energy: The Real Bottlenecks for AI Engineers

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 26th November, 2026 · 2 min read

If you’re deploying AI, you’ve felt the crunch: GPU pricing is volatile, and custom inference chips are starting to dominate. The news cycle is full of NVIDIA H100 shortages, but the real story is datacenter energy and supply chain risk. Engineers need to rethink how (and where) they ship models.

Custom Silicon Is Eating the World

Google’s TPUv6, Meta’s Artemis, and Amazon’s Inferentia 3 are now in production, and they’re cheap for inference. This isn’t just vendor marketing: if you want to ship AI at scale, you must optimize for LLMs and vision models on whatever silicon is cheapest. The days of "just use CUDA" are over—engineers who don’t profile for custom chips are burning both money and latency.

Energy and Location

Energy is the new bottleneck. Datacenters are maxing out grid capacity in Virginia, Sweden, and even India. Engineers need to care because inference costs are now dominated by energy, not silicon. It matters whether your model runs in Mumbai or Dallas—latency and cost vary by local grid and chip supply. Smart teams are using geo-routing and hybrid deployment (mixing cloud and edge) to chase cheaper power.

Shipping Decisions

The best engineers are benchmarking their LLMs on both GPU and custom chip endpoints before launch. If you’re still hardcoding CUDA, you’re missing out on 20–50% cost savings. If you don’t know your datacenter’s grid constraints, expect outages or throttling.

Bottom line: custom silicon and datacenter energy are now as important as your model architecture. Profile, benchmark, and route—don’t just deploy.
