Agentic Coding in Practice: Claude Code, Loop Engineering, and Spec-Driven Workflows

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 29th October, 2026 · 2 min read

Let’s get real about how developers are actually using AI day-to-day. This week, Anthropic’s Claude Code is the standout: devs are running plan→act→verify→fix loops, not just for debugging, but for full agentic flows. That means you’re letting the AI plan multi-step changes, execute them in a sandbox, verify outcomes, and iterate. Why does this matter? Because the old ‘prompt and pray’ model is dead.

Spec-Driven Prompting Is Table Stakes

The serious devs are now writing specs first—think contract-level descriptions, not just natural language. You feed these into the agent to set boundaries, expectations, and acceptance tests. Doing this cuts hallucinations and keeps outputs on target. If your AI can’t pass your spec, it loops until it does. This is eval-driven development in practice.

MCP Tool Servers and the Rise of Pair-Programming Agents

Multi-Command Protocol (MCP) tool servers are everywhere. They let agents interact with your local tools, APIs, and cloud endpoints, not just in a vacuum. The upshot: devs are running AI pair-programming sessions where the agent writes code, runs tests, fetches data, and even files PRs—all within a tight feedback loop. It’s not about replacing engineers, it’s about scaling them.

Why Engineers Should Care

If you’re still using AI as a glorified autocomplete, you’re missing the point. The real value is in agentic loops—plan→act→verify→fix—tied to clear specs and continuous evaluation. It’s faster, more reliable, and lets you ship side projects daily. Don’t wait for ‘enterprise adoption’—these workflows are already boosting velocity and quality for indie devs and startups.
