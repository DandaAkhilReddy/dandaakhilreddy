Agentic Loops, Spec-Driven Prompts, and the New AI Pairing Reality

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 27th November, 2026 · 2 min read

There's a shift happening in how we write code, and it's more profound than the leap from Vim to VSCode. The big news: agentic coding is moving from hype to hands-on. With tools like Claude Code and Mosaic's LLM agents, we're not just getting code suggestions—AI is running full plan-act-verify-fix loops on real production tasks. This matters because it’s a radically different workflow: you describe a spec, the agent decomposes, implements, tests, and iterates autonomously, and you review.

Loop Engineering In Practice

The most effective teams I see now start with a spec-driven prompt. You lay out the problem (say, 'add OAuth2 to our Go backend'), hand it to Claude or a local open-weight agent, and let it plan the steps. Each step's code, tests, and even review comments are generated in a loop: plan → act → verify → fix. If evals fail, the agent self-corrects—no more 'just autocomplete my function' baby steps.

MCP Tool Servers and Eval-Driven Dev

To make agentic coding reliable, people are running MCP tool servers (think: an API surface for the AI to call your linters, test runners, and build tools) and integrating with eval-driven development—automatic checks on every suggestion, with AI refactoring based on test outcomes. This isn't theoretical: I shipped two features this week by pairing with an eval-first agent, spending more time writing specs and less time fighting with stack traces.

Why It Matters

If you’re an engineer looking for leverage, agentic loops and spec-driven prompts put you closer to the 'describe the what, let the AI handle the how' workflow. The bottleneck shifts to clear specs and good test coverage, but that's a trade worth making. The best AI devs in 2026 are already spending half their day pairing—sometimes competitively—with an agent, and the productivity jump is real.
