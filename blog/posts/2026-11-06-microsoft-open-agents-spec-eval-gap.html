Microsoft’s Copilot Gap: Why Open Agent APIs and Spec-Eval Loops Can’t Wait

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 6th November, 2026 · 2 min read

I keep seeing the same feedback: Copilot is powerful, but it’s still a black box. Meanwhile, Anthropic and Google are shipping open agent APIs, spec-driven workflows, and first-class eval tooling. Their agents integrate anywhere, run plan-act-verify loops, and accept external tool calls. Why aren’t we doing the same?

Open Agents, Not Walled Copilots

If Microsoft wants Copilot and Azure AI to matter to engineers in 2026, we need an open agent API—not just chats and inline code. Give me composable agents that I can orchestrate, audit, and connect to my own toolchains. Hard requirements: external memory, tool/plugin support, and full API access to the plan, act, and eval steps—not just "suggest code".

Spec-Eval Loops as First-Class Citizens

The next-gen dev workflow is spec-driven and eval-first. Copilot should own the entire plan→act→verify→fix loop, letting devs inject custom evals, see agent reasoning, and roll back or patch autonomously. The current "take or leave" suggestions miss the bigger picture: engineers want repeatable, testable, spec-aligned automation, not just faster typing.

We’re at risk of falling behind if we don’t act. Let’s open up Copilot and Azure agents, make agentic workflows programmable, and double down on transparent, auditable eval loops. That’s what developers—and the future of software—demand.
