Open Foundry Chips and Inference Wars: What the New AI Silicon Landscape Means for Us

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 22nd October, 2026 · 2 min read

The AI chip wars have a new player: open foundries. For the first time, multiple startups (plus China’s big silicon players) are taping out custom AI inference chips without Nvidia or TSMC in the loop. Why should you care? Because it means we’ll see inference costs drop—but not everywhere, and not for every use case.

GPUs, TPUs, and Custom Silicon: The New Reality

Nvidia’s H200s are still king for multi-modal, >100B parameter models, but inference APIs (from Cohere, Groq, even Google’s Trillium) are now quoting sub-$0.10/1M tokens for standard LLMs. The catch: You have to re-write your model runners or even change your operator graph to target new silicon.

Datacenter Build-Outs and Supply Chain Chaos

Data center build-outs are still frenetic—Microsoft and Google can drop $10B on a new region, but smaller players are betting on specialist silicon and local inference clusters. Open foundry chips (see Rivos, Tenstorrent, and others) mean some teams are spinning up inference-only racks at 1/5th the energy per token—but only if your codebase can target their SDK.

What Engineers Need to Do Now

Portability is existential. If you’re not writing with an abstraction layer (or at least containerizing your runners so you can swap chips), you’re going to get crushed by cost curves by 2027. Also, expect more outages and supply hiccups—new silicon means new bottlenecks, and SRE isn’t ready.

Why it matters: If you ship AI at scale, silicon choices are now product choices. Infer cheaper, infer local, or get priced out.
