DANDA #101 — CourtroomAI: End Evidence Presentation Fumbles and Tech Failures in Court

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 30th October, 2026 · 4 min read

Every year, U.S. courtrooms waste countless hours due to lost, mislabeled, or incompatible evidence files and technical breakdowns during trials. Judges, attorneys, and clerks struggle with PDF errors, missing metadata, faulty projectors, and last-second exhibit confusion. The backlog is measured not just in time, but in mistrials, delayed justice, lost records, and avoidable appeals.

The problem

Over 40 million court cases are filed in the U.S. every year. At least 20% of trial hours are spent on non-substantive procedural and technical issues, with digital evidence presentation a major bottleneck. Courts have spent billions digitizing records, but standardization is lacking: over 70% of U.S. courts cannot natively handle key file types (video, medical images, proprietary formats), forcing lawyers and clerks to rely on ad hoc conversion tools. Lost or corrupted exhibits lead to delays and, in the worst cases, grounds for appeals or mistrials. A single delayed major trial can cost a city $20,000–$50,000 in extra personnel and facility costs. The true cost: justice delayed, public money wasted, and outcomes at risk for millions each year.

The idea: CourtroomAI

"CourtroomAI" is an autonomous agent platform that sits between evidence submitters (lawyers, police, expert witnesses) and the courtroom's AV systems and case management software. It auto-ingests all digital evidence, validates format, does real-time compatibility checks, standardizes metadata, and orchestrates seamless, secure presentation for every hearing. Before trial, it flags incompatibilities and missing files, creates a unified evidence timeline, and generates a human-auditable readiness report. During trial, it acts as a responsive copilot: bringing up exhibits on command, logging every display event, and monitoring for technical failures. It even provides live feedback to the judge if presentation issues threaten the record or fairness. The result: 100% compatible, auditable, and orderly evidence — every time.

Architecture

Every piece of evidence from attorneys or officers flows into the Ingestion layer, which scans for format, completeness, and compatibility. The Memory/Graph module builds a timeline and tracks every version and metadata link. The Agent Orchestrator schedules and manages presentation, while the Human Gate (via judge/clerk dashboard) allows for human approval, override, or manual cueing. The Action Layer runs AV control, live screens, and real-time logs. Errors or tech issues are routed back to the Orchestrator and surfaced to the Human Gate for immediate mitigation, closing the loop that currently breaks down in real courtrooms.

Build plan (90 days)

Wedge: Focus on county and state trial courts with high digital evidence volume (criminal, civil). Start with a web dashboard, evidence upload, and pre-trial compatibility validation. Stack:

  • Day 0–30: Secure court IT pilot partners; ship ingestion, file scanning, and PDF/video validation modules. Integrate with one major case management system (e.g., Tyler).
  • Day 31–60: Layer in timeline graph, error-checking, and judge/clerk UI. Add first AV hardware API integration (projector, screen, recording).
  • Day 61–90: Live courtroom orchestration, full event logging, and audit reporting. Launch with two pilot courts. SOC2/Justice data compliance from day one.
Pricing: SaaS, $2,500/mo/courtroom, all-inclusive (evidence/AV volume upcharges for large metropolitan courts).

Why now

Courtrooms are finally digitized, but the fragmentation is severe — and trials are longer and more expensive than ever due to technical chaos, not substance. Generative AI and real-time agentic orchestration make it possible to finally connect messy human input, legacy systems, and rigid AV hardware. Courts are under pressure to clear backlogs, cut costs, and prevent mistrials. CourtroomAI lands at the right moment: fast to pilot, cost-justified by time savings alone, and mission-critical for justice.
