Agentic Coding: Beyond Prompts, Into Loops, Specs, and Eval-Driven Dev

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 20th November, 2026 · 2 min read

If you’re building with AI in late 2026, you’re probably not slinging one-off prompts anymore. The news: Claude Code just shipped a tighter plan-act-verify loop API, and devs are wiring agents to MCP tool servers for bespoke workflows.

Agentic Coding: Real Loops, Not Just Chat

What matters is that these agents now plan out a coding task, act by writing or refactoring, verify via evals or tests, and then fix based on failures. Engineers are chaining these loops—not just for coding, but for CI/CD, infra, and even doc generation. You see this in the wild with Claude’s new loop APIs and Anthropic’s spec-driven prompting, where you feed a formal spec or test suite and the agent iterates until passing.

MCP Tool Servers: Bring Your Own Toolchain

The rise of MCP tool servers means you aren’t stuck with the agent’s built-in tools. You expose your dev tools, infra scripts, or even linters as API endpoints—agents call them, verify output, and adapt. This flexibility is critical for engineers: your stack, your conventions, your testing philosophy.

Eval-Driven Development

Eval-driven development is now table stakes. Devs set up evals (unit tests, scenario checks, static analysis), agents run them continuously as part of the loop, and you get a feedback-driven dev cycle. This reduces hallucinations and helps agents align to your org’s quality bar. When you pair-program with AI, it’s not about auto-completion—it’s about collaborative, spec-first workflows.

Why does this matter? Because it’s finally closing the gap between “AI as an assistant” and “AI as a reliable, test-driven coder.” If you care about shipping robust code, you want agents that follow spec, adapt to feedback, and play nice with your tools.
