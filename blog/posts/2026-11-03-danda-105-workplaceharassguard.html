DANDA #105 — WorkplaceHarassGuard: AI Agents to End HR Blind Spots in Workplace Harassment Reporting

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 3rd November, 2026 · 4 min read

Every year, millions of employees endure workplace harassment but never report it, fearing retaliation, disbelief, or bureaucratic HR dead ends. These cases fester in silence, eroding trust and culture while exposing employers to legal, reputational, and financial disaster. Today’s "open door" policies and inbox-based reporting are broken — it’s time for an AI-native platform that guarantees every voice is heard, acted on, and never lost in the shuffle.

The problem

Workplace harassment is rampant and systematically underreported. The EEOC estimates that up to 75% of all workplace harassment incidents are never formally reported. Of those that are, 68% say nothing changes or things get worse. The result: $2.6 billion in annual settlements in the US alone, not counting reputational damage or the estimated 10 million lost workdays per year due to harassment-related stress and turnover. Traditional reporting channels (HR email, phone, in-person) are intimidating, slow, and opaque for victims, while HR teams drown in manual casework and risk missing critical details or deadlines. For companies, one missed complaint can mean a viral lawsuit or employee exodus overnight.

The idea: WorkplaceHarassGuard

I’m building WorkplaceHarassGuard: autonomous AI agents that automate, anonymize, and track workplace harassment reporting. Employees can report by chat, voice, or web — anonymized if desired — with the agent guiding them through structured, trauma-informed intake. The agent automatically generates compliant records, flags urgency, cross-references patterns (repeat offenders, high-risk departments), and guides HR (or external counsel) through timely, auditable resolution steps. Every incident is logged, tracked, and never buried. For employees, it’s safe, easy, and transparent. For HR, it’s automation, alerts, and real accountability — no more blind spots or compliance risk.

Architecture

The flow: Employees submit reports via chat, voice, or web portal (Inputs). The Ingestion layer immediately anonymizes data (if requested), parses responses, and checks for completeness. Cases are routed into a secure Memory/Graph layer that stores all incidents, links them to prior cases, and runs pattern analysis for escalation triggers. The Agent Orchestrator manages intake dialogue, triage, and routing — prompting for missing info and recommending next steps. At the Human Gate, HR or legal teams are prompted for review and intervention. The Action Layer handles notifications, documentation, and creates immutable audit trails for compliance and follow-up. Every step is visible, logged, and never lost.

Build plan (90 days)

Wedge: Target US companies 250–2000 employees (where HR risk is high but internal tools are weak). First, deliver AI-powered harassment intake and tracking for HR teams; focus on anonymous chat/voice, instant compliance logs, and alerts for repeat offenders or missed deadlines.
Stack: Add real-time analytics, pattern analysis (across locations/departments), and integration with HRIS and external legal advisory. Offer employee mobile app for direct reporting. Expand to multilingual support and external ombudsman workflows.
Pricing: Subscription, $3–$6/employee/month (tiered by size), plus premium for AI-driven pattern reports and compliance bundles. Guarantee reduction in average response time and increase in case closure rate.

Why now

Regulators are tightening workplace harassment reporting requirements, and brand risk is at an all-time high — one missed case can tank recruiting and spark class actions overnight. Employees expect safe, tech-native channels and instant transparency, not ancient inboxes or HR hotlines. AI agents can deliver trauma-informed, 24/7, confidential reporting at scale, closing the gap between policy and reality. The companies who adopt this now will lead on safety, culture, and brand — and never get blindsided again.
