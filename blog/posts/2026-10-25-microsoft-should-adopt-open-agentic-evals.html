If Microsoft Wants Serious AI Devs, It Needs Open Agentic APIs and Eval-Driven Workflows

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 25th October, 2026 · 2 min read

I’m seeing a clear gap: OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google all just rolled out open agentic APIs—you can plug in your own tools, create agentic workflows, and even swap out eval suites. Meanwhile, Microsoft’s Copilot stack? It’s still a black box. Sure, it’s useful, but it’s not programmable in the way real engineers want.

What Microsoft Needs to Learn (Right Now)

First, open up agentic APIs across Copilot, GitHub, and Azure AI. Let engineers define toolchains—maybe even run their own MCP servers—and wire up custom loops. Anthropic’s Claude Code shows this is not just possible, but necessary: teams want to tune, test, and trust what their agents are doing, and that requires deep hooks, not just surface-level prompts.

Second, Microsoft needs to adopt eval-driven workflows as a first-class citizen. This means: APIs for plugging in eval suites, surfacing every pass/fail, and making agent behaviors legible—not just "here’s your code, hope it works." OpenAI’s eval APIs and Anthropic’s eval/verify loops are setting the bar. Microsoft has the infra and scale, but is behind on developer trust and extensibility.

Why This Actually Matters

Real AI engineers want to own their workflow. They want to know why the agent chose what it did, inject custom tools, and run battle-hardened evals. If Microsoft wants to avoid losing the next generation of devs (especially those burned by Copilot’s "magical" misses), it needs to get out of the closed magic box and into the open agentic ecosystem. Ship the APIs, open the evals, and let engineers build with Copilot, not just using it.
