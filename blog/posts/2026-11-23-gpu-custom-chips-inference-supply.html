Custom Silicon, Inference Costs, and the Scramble for Supply

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 23rd November, 2026 · 2 min read

Every engineering team deploying AI is feeling it: the supply chain for compute is still tight, but the market is fragmenting. Nvidia’s H100X2 is everywhere in the news, but access is patchy, and costs are sticking. At the same time, Google just released details (and open drivers) for TPUv7, making vertical AI deployment less of a walled garden than before. Meanwhile, Groq and Tenstorrent are pushing their own silicon into the cloud via partnerships.

Inference Costs: Still Spiky, But Getting Smarter

The real shift is in inference economics. Yes, on-demand GPU prices are high, but toolchains are finally catching up with model quantization, speculative decoding, and smart routing. That means teams can run more on smaller nodes, and even spin up inference on custom edge silicon (think: automotive or on-prem).

Supply Chain: Two Worlds

For US/EU hyperscalers, the supply chain is stabilizing, but Asia-Pacific teams are still feeling squeeze—thanks to export controls and local demand. Why does this matter? Because as open-source LLMs (like Llama 4 and Gemma 3B) get better, more teams will run their own models—and hardware choice becomes a strategic decision, not just 'grab the next A100'.

What to Watch

If you’re building for scale, start investing in profiling and quantization pipelines—don’t just chase the latest GPU. Evaluate TPUs (if you’re GCP-native) or emerging chips, since vendor lock-in is getting more expensive. And keep an eye on inference cost calculators—not just list prices, but real end-to-end costs including energy and networking. Compute is the new bottleneck, and the teams who solve for it will win.
