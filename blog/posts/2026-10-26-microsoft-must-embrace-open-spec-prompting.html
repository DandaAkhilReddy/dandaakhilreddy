Why Microsoft Needs Open Spec-Prompting and Loop APIs — Lessons From Anthropic and Google

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 26th October, 2026 · 2 min read

If you're building with Microsoft Copilot or Azure's AI tools, you've probably hit the wall: there's no open API for spec-driven prompting, agentic loop orchestration, or integrating eval harnesses. Anthropic (Claude Code) and Google (Vertex AI tool orchestration) are racing ahead with open, programmable interfaces that let engineers build their own loops and workflows.

Why Closed APIs Slow Us Down

Right now, Copilot is still a black box. You can't easily plug in custom evaluation metrics, tool APIs, or orchestrate complex loops across files, tests, and workspace tools. For engineers, this means we're stuck with rigid workflows and can't automate the kinds of agentic loops that are proven to speed up development.

What Microsoft Should Learn

Anthropic's Claude Code lets you define specs, expose workspace APIs, and run eval-driven loops—so you can instrument your own coding flows. Google Vertex runs tool servers that you can attach to agents, enabling evals and fixes as part of the workflow. This isn't just 'nice to have'. It's foundational for serious devs.

Concrete Steps

Microsoft needs to open up Copilot and Azure AI APIs for spec-driven prompting, expose loop orchestration endpoints, and let us plug in custom eval harnesses. It means more developer control, better debugging, and faster iteration. If Microsoft doesn't, engineers will keep jumping ship to more open platforms.

For engineers, this matters because open APIs let us shape our tools around our workflows—not the other way around. It's time for Microsoft to get serious about agentic coding, not just chat-centric AI.
