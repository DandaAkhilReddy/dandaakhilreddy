If Microsoft Wants To Lead in AI, It Needs Eval-First Workflows and Open Agentic APIs

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 24th October, 2026 · 2 min read

Microsoft is in the pole position when it comes to AI for developers: Copilot in every IDE, Azure AI endpoints, even GitHub Next tooling. But here’s the uncomfortable truth: they’re falling behind on agentic workflows. The labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) are shipping open APIs for tool access, eval hooks, and agentic loop control. Copilot? Still a black box.

Eval-First, Not Magic Black Box

If you want engineers to trust AI, you need to give them control over evaluation. Anthropic, OpenAI, and even Google’s new models are exposing eval-APIs that let devs inject their own checks, tests, and loop controls directly into the inference flow. Microsoft needs to build this into Copilot and Azure—let users wire up custom evaluators, not just hit “accept” on autocomplete.

Open Agentic APIs: Let Devs Build, Don’t Just Demo

The future isn’t “prompt and pray.” It’s agents that plan, act, verify, and fix—in tight, testable cycles. Microsoft has the infra and the user base, but it needs to open up agentic APIs (the way Anthropic’s Claude and OpenAI’s Assistants API just did). Let devs connect trusted tools, run code in sandboxes, and monitor/interrupt the agent’s plan. This isn’t just for power users; it’s how you move from toy to tool, from demo to dev workflow.

GitHub, Foundry, and the Opportunity

Foundry and GitHub Next could be the frontlines for this shift—if they start exposing the right hooks. Imagine Copilot that can run your custom tests, refactor against your eval scripts, and even call your own sandbox tools, all in one loop. That’s the bar now. The competition is moving fast.

Bottom Line

Engineers don’t want magic—they want control, transparency, and testability. If Microsoft wants to stay ahead, it needs to build eval-first, agentic APIs into everything Copilot touches. Otherwise, someone else will.
