GPU Supply Crunch and Inference Costs: Why Compute Is Now Your Bottleneck

AR Akhil Reddy Danda · 26th October, 2026 · 2 min read

The news everyone is talking about: NVIDIA H200 chips are sold out until Q2 2027, and Google just announced ultra-efficient TPUs aimed at inference. Datacenter operators are scrambling to build out capacity, but energy prices and supply chain snarls mean slower rollouts and higher costs.

Why GPUs Are Still King (and a Pain)

For AI engineers, this matters because GPUs are still the backbone of model deployment and inference. When you can't get H200s, you're stuck either scaling horizontally (more, older GPUs) or optimizing your models for custom silicon—like Google's new TPUs or AWS's Inferentia. This often means rewriting parts of your pipeline, re-optimizing code, and dealing with vendor lock-in.

Energy and Inference Costs

Here's the kicker: inference costs are not dropping. In fact, with energy prices up and chip shortages, we're seeing per-inference pricing rise. Engineers can't ignore this. If you're serving millions of requests, even small efficiency gains (quantization, pruning, caching) matter. Shipping AI is as much about tuning compute as model quality.

Datacenter Build-Outs and Supply Chain

Microsoft, Google, AWS, everyone is racing to expand datacenters. But silicon shortages (substrates, packaging) and grid constraints mean timelines slip. For devs, this means you need to plan for hardware diversity—optimizing for whatever chips are available, not just hoping NVIDIA comes through.

Bottom line: compute is your bottleneck. Engineers have to watch inference costs, energy use, and chip supply as closely as they watch their code. If you're not, you're risking outages, blown budgets, and slower ships.
